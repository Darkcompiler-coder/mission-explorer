03 / Deep space

Some journeys continue after the close encounters

Planetary probes may carry on along their original trajectories after their primary science targets. A signal can fade, a spacecraft can be deliberately disposed of, or a probe can be left at a destination it was built to reach.

Sort the endings

Silent, still travelling, or intentionally placed?

These are different mission outcomes. A spacecraft in interstellar space is not the same as a lander on a moon, and a controlled end-of-mission maneuver is not an accidental abandonment.

No longer communicating

Pioneer 10 and 11

Launched 1972 and 1973 · outward solar-system trajectories

Pioneer 10 was the first spacecraft to make close observations of Jupiter. Pioneer 11 later visited Jupiter and Saturn. Both carried plaques describing humans and the origin of the spacecraft.

Current situation: Their signals ended decades ago. The spacecraft continue on trajectories away from the Sun, but they are not heading toward a specific star on a useful timescale.

Still communicating

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2

Launched 1977 · interstellar space

The twin probes visited the outer planets, and both crossed the heliosphere’s boundary into interstellar space. Their instruments still return limited data while power declines.

Message from Earth: Each spacecraft carries a Golden Record with sounds, images and greetings selected to represent life on Earth.

Cassini ended deliberately

Cassini and Huygens

Saturn and Titan · 1997–2017 / 2005

NASA’s Cassini orbiter studied Saturn for 13 years. Its Huygens probe descended through Titan’s atmosphere and landed on the moon’s surface in 2005.

Two different endings: Huygens remains at Titan. Cassini was directed into Saturn in 2017 to protect potentially habitable moons from contamination.

Mission continues

New Horizons

Launched 2006 · Kuiper Belt

New Horizons flew past Pluto in 2015 and Arrokoth in 2019, returning the first close-up views of both. It continues observing the distant Solar System as power allows.

On board: The spacecraft carries part of the ashes of Pluto discoverer Clyde Tombaugh, along with scientific instruments and a small U.S. flag.

A note on “interstellar”

Outside the heliosphere is not the same as leaving the galaxy

Voyager crossed the heliosphere—the large bubble shaped by the solar wind and magnetic field. It remains gravitationally associated with the Sun and is still within the Milky Way. “Interstellar space” describes the region beyond the heliosphere, not a journey out of our galaxy.
